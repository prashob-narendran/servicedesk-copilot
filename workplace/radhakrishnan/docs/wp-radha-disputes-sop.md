# Workplace SOP – Card and Electronic Payment Disputes

Illustrative procedure written for the Situated Learning section. It reflects common practice in US retail banking (Regulation E for electronic fund transfers, Regulation Z for credit cards) and contains no confidential bank content. Always follow the bank's approved policy.

## Information to collect on every dispute call

1. Verify the caller's identity using the bank's call-centre verification steps.
2. Record the transaction date, amount, merchant or ATM location, and the channel (ATM, debit card, credit card, Zelle).
3. Ask whether the customer still has the card and whether the transaction was authorised by them.
4. Never ask for or record the full card number, PIN, CVV or one-time passcodes.
5. For suspected fraud, block the card immediately and order a replacement.

## ATM and debit card disputes (electronic fund transfers)

An ATM that debits the account without dispensing cash is an error under electronic fund transfer rules. Open a dispute case the same day. If the investigation is not finished within 10 business days, give the customer provisional credit; the investigation may then take up to 45 days. Tell the customer the outcome in writing.

## Credit card disputes

Unauthorised charges and billing errors (including duplicate charges) are opened as card dispute cases. Acknowledge the dispute in writing within 30 days and resolve it within two billing cycles, and in any case within 90 days. The customer does not pay the disputed amount while the case is open.

## Zelle and other person-to-person payments

Payments the customer did not authorise are handled as unauthorised electronic transfers, like ATM and debit disputes. Payments the customer authorised, including payments to a seller who turned out to be a scammer, are generally not unauthorised transfers. Open a scam case, contact the receiving bank to try to recover the funds, and review the case under the bank's scam-reimbursement policy. Do not promise a refund on the call.
